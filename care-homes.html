Why oral care matters in long-term care

Care comes to the resident: in their own chair, in a room they know, with the time and patience the visit deserves.

The need continues with age
Good oral health stays important as a person ages, becomes chronically ill, or moves into a care residence.
High rates of oral disease
Many older adults who rely on others for care have poor oral hygiene and high rates of oral disease.
Pain and difficulty eating
Oral disease causes pain and trouble chewing and swallowing, which affects nutrition and quality of life.
Links to whole-body health
Poor oral health is associated with heart and lung disease, diabetes and stroke.

Source: Canadian Dental Hygienists Association.

Care that fits into your day

Designed around residents, families and your team’s routines.

No transport required
All clinical equipment comes on site, so residents never leave the building.
Minimal staff involvement
I coordinate with residents and families where appropriate, keeping demands on your team light.
Dementia-informed care
Unhurried appointments with frequent breaks and familiar surroundings.
Fewer downstream issues
Regular hygiene lowers the risk of pain, infection and aspiration pneumonia linked to poor oral health.
Clear documentation
Written notes after every visit for your records, plus referrals when further treatment is needed.
Flexible scheduling
Visits arranged around meals and activities, including recurring monthly or quarterly rounds.

No cost to your facility

Each resident is billed individually through their own coverage, whether that’s NIHB, the Canadian Dental Care Plan, ODSP or private insurance, with direct billing wherever possible. Your facility carries no administrative or financial burden, and families receive clear documentation for every visit.

How a partnership works

It starts with a conversation, not paperwork. Residents and families are included from the first visit, and your team stays informed at every step.

  1. Introductory call

    A short conversation about your residents, your routines and the support that would help most.

  2. On-site assessment visit

    A walkthrough to review the space, meet your team and see which residents would benefit.

  3. Consent and coverage

    Consent and medical history are collected for each resident, and coverage is confirmed in advance.

  4. Ongoing visits

    Care begins on an agreed schedule, with notes after each visit and adjustments as needs change.
