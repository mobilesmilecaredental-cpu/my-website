Dental hygiene for children

A cleaning at home, on your child’s terms — no waiting room, no rush, and one-on-one time to make it genuinely fun.

No waiting room!
Brush along!

  • One-on-one attention with nothing rushed
  • A familiar, playful setting instead of a clinic chair

Children's hygiene care, without the trip

For busy parents, a cleaning shouldn't mean an afternoon off work, a car seat battle, and a waiting room full of strangers. Care happens in your living room, on a schedule that fits around school and naps.

Because it's one-on-one with no one waiting, there's room to make it genuinely engaging. Children get plenty of hands-on oral hygiene instruction, brushing and flossing practice they can actually keep up, and time to ask questions until they feel comfortable.

By age one The First Visit

Children should have their first dental visit within six months of their first tooth appearing, or by their first birthday, whichever comes first.

Why early visits matter

Catch problems early

Early signs of tooth decay or improper jaw development can be spotted long before they become painful or costly.

Build comfort

Early, gentle visits help your child feel safe and relaxed about dental care instead of anxious about it.

Guidance for parents

You’ll learn the best ways to clean tiny teeth and how to manage habits like thumb-sucking.
